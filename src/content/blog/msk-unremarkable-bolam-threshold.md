## The threshold in everyday language

A routine knee radiograph reported as "unremarkable" is not, on its own, a breach
of duty — even where a later MRI reveals pathology the plain film could not be
expected to show. The Bolam test is about whether the reporting radiologist
acted in accordance with a practice accepted as proper by a responsible body of
radiological opinion. It is not decided by the benefit of hindsight.

## Where the difficulty lies

The trap for the plaintiff is the word *unremarkable*. It is a shorthand for "I
looked and found nothing I need to escalate". In an MSK context that claim must
be **verifiable** — the reporter should be able to point to the series and the
abnormalities (or clear absence of them) that justified the conclusion.

Where the images actually demonstrate a lesion and the report states the
contrary, the shield of Bolam is harder to sustain, because no responsible body
of opinion would properly describe a demonstrable fracture as unremarkable.

## The practical takeaway

For experts asked to opine on a routine report, the disciplined method is:

1. re-derive from the source images,
2. identify whether the finding in dispute is visible on the series actually reviewed,
3. opine on **conduct and standard**, separately from **outcome and causation**.

This keeps the threshold analysis honest and the report admissible under
CPR Part 35.
