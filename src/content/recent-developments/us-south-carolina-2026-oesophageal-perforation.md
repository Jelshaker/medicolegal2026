## **South Carolina Wrongful Death Verdict (Over the pond)**

**State Court, Columbia, South Carolina, United States**  
**Two-week jury trial — Verdict: $24 million (including >$14.6m non-economic damages)**

### Facts and imaging

A 36-year-old commercial pilot and former F-16 fighter pilot underwent a catheter ablation procedure for atrial fibrillation. He experienced persistent symptoms for approximately one month post-procedure, prompting his clinicians to request an investigative **CT angiogram**.

- **Radiological interpretation:** The reporting radiologist classified the CT angiogram as normal, failing to identify a critical **oesophageal perforation**.
- **Clinical outcome:** Because the perforation went unrecognised and untreated, the patient deteriorated and died 12 days after the scan.
- **Prior settlement:** Before trial against the radiologist, the estate reached a $1.2 million settlement with the interventional cardiologist who performed the initial ablation.

### Outcome and significance

Following a two-week trial, the South Carolina jury returned a **$24 million** wrongful death verdict. While US damages awards reflect state tort rules and lay jury assessment rather than UK quantum principles, the clinical lesson is universal: in post-ablation patients presenting with persistent thoracic or systemic symptoms, the mediastinum and oesophagus require targeted review for perforation or fistulation.
