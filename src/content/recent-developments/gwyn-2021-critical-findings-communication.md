**Gwyn v South Tyneside and Sunderland NHS Foundation Trust**

[2021] (unreported — no neutral citation; verify before reliance)

## Issue

fail-safe reporting of critical findings.

## Facts

incidental nodule suspicious for malignancy reported but not directly communicated. No alert reached the treating team.

## Principle

duty extends beyond interpretation to effective communication. A report filed silently on an electronic system does not discharge duty where urgent action is required. Closed loop notification is required.

## Why it matters — radiology

Gwyn is the fail-safe case: the error was not perception but transmission — an abnormal result filed where nobody acted on it. It converts "I reported it" from a complete defence into the start of the duty: the result must reach someone who will act.

## For consultant radiologists

design reports for acknowledgement — critical and unexpected findings need direct communication, documented receipt, and escalation where acknowledgement fails. The RCR Standards' acknowledgement and addendum procedures exist precisely for this claim pattern.

## For legal professionals

Gwyn claims are proved through IT and administrative records — filing logs, acknowledgement audits, inbox cover — rather than image review. Disclosure should target the communication chain end to end.
