## Background

The claimant presented to A&E with acute lower back pain, urinary urgency and
unilateral calf weakness. A same-day MRI lumbar spine was reported as
"mild disc protrusion at L4/5, no compressive lesion identified". Surgery was
referred onwards, but the clinical team did not act on the referral for three
further days. By the time the lumbar decompression was performed, the functional
outcome had deteriorated materially.

Subsequent expert review identified a large sequestrated disc fragment at the
L5/S1 level with crowding of the descending cauda equina fibres — findings not
recorded in the original report.

## The radiological question

The dispute centred on whether the original reporting radiologist fell below the
standard of a reasonably competent practitioner, and — separately — whether an
earlier operation would have materially altered the claimant's trajectory.

In my re-derivation I tabulated three things:

- the imaging **protocol** actually performed (axial T2, sagittal T2, axial T1),
- the **size and location** of the sequestrum relative to the nerve root
  (dural sac compression demonstrated on two orthogonal planes),
- the **clinical correlation** a same-day report should reasonably have drawn
  given the documented neurology.

## Opinion

The images themselves were diagnostic. The omission was not a subtle artefact or
a modality limitation; it was a completed, recursive review that failed to
register an objectively demonstrable mass. In CPR Part 35 terms, the report
should record the limits of the opinion and the assumptions upon which it rests.
Here, the primary assumption — that no compressive lesion existed — was
contradicted by the images in front of the reporter.

I therefore framed the breach as a *reporting* failure rather than an imaging
failure, which materially assisted the litigator in isolating the causation
analysis from the radiology.

## Practical note for referrers

When instructing in cauda equina cases, ask specifically for:

1. re-derivation from the source DICOM, not re-reading a report,
2. an explicit table of protocol coverage,
3. a statement on whether the compressed sac was visible on the images that
   were expressed to be reviewed on the initial reporting date.
