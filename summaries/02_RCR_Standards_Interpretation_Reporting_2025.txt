================================================================================
SUMMARY: Royal College of Radiologists (RCR) - Standards for Interpretation
         and Reporting of Imaging Investigations (3rd Edition, October 2025)
SOURCE: The Royal College of Radiologists, Clinical Radiology
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
This is the definitive UK standard document governing the interpretation and
reporting of imaging investigations. Published in October 2025 (3rd Edition),
it represents the current expected standard of care for radiological reporting.
The document is written by and for radiologists but applies to ALL who interpret
and report imaging, regardless of professional background.

KEY PRINCIPLES
--------------
- Radiological investigations must be interpreted and reported to a high standard
- Clear communication of results ensures safe, effective, efficient patient care
- Modern radiology services are under pressure due to workforce shortages
- Imaging reporting services must remain led by consultant radiologists but will
  not be entirely radiologist-delivered
- Non-radiology doctors interpreting imaging must have agreements for radiology
  review when findings fall outside their expertise
- Non-medical practitioners (sonographers, radiographers) must work within
  consultant radiologist-led teams with clear supervision
- Referrers have legal duties under IR(ME)R to provide adequate patient histories
  and articulate clear clinical questions

SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION
-----------------------
Defines the scope and purpose of the standards. Establishes that:
- All who interpret and report imaging must adhere to these standards
- Where formal reports are provided by non-radiology doctors (e.g., cardiologists),
  the same standards apply
- Where imaging interpretation is delegated to referrers (e.g., orthopaedic
  surgeons), written documentation must satisfy IR(ME)R requirements
- High-quality referrals are essential for safe care

SECTION 2: SERVICE ORGANISATION
-------------------------------
Providers must create and regularly review standard operating procedures for:
- Turnaround times
- Communication of unexpected or time-critical findings
- Imaging classification (critical, urgent, routine, research trial) for reporting priority
- Communication of addenda and MDT reviews
- Report acknowledgement

Principles:
- Patient safety requires timely, accurate, actionable reporting
- Standards apply to anyone providing a report and all providers

SECTION 3: REPORTING AUTHOR
----------------------------
The report must:
- Clearly state the name, job title/professional status, registration body, and
  registration number of the author
- The prefix 'Doctor' or 'Dr' should only indicate medically qualified practitioners
- Non-medical practitioners with PhD should use 'PhD' as postnominal, not 'Dr'
- Not include personal contact details
- Include names of ALL contributing authors
- For interventional procedures, author should be operator or assistant; primary
  operator must be clearly indicated

Employer duties:
- Duty of care to patients
- No employee permitted/required to work beyond knowledge and competence
- No employee required to work without adequate rest

Reporting author requirements:
- Registered with relevant UK regulatory body (GMC, HCPC) and in good standing
- Trained per professional body's standards
- Working within defined scope of practice
- Comply with ongoing CPD, audit, and revalidation processes
- Able to evaluate image quality and suitability for diagnosis
- Must recognise suboptimal image quality and include caveats when needed

SECTION 4: INFRASTRUCTURE
-------------------------
Technology Support:
- Patient's imaging history and reports must be available
- Clinical information, medical records, and laboratory results should be accessible
- Brief controlled delay (up to 2 minutes) before report becomes accessible
  (to allow typographical corrections)
- Once report accessed by another party, changes require formal version control
- Reports must be automatically communicable to other clinician IT systems

Voice Recognition:
- VR software is integral to reporting and encouraged
- Employer must ensure VR software and hardware perform to current best standards
- Typing or alternative methods may be used if no less effective than high-quality VR

Artificial Intelligence Assisted Reporting:
- AI has potential to significantly improve healthcare through analysis of complex
  information at scale
- AI can aid throughout the patient journey: requesting, scheduling, vetting,
  planning, scanning, report sharing, treatment pathway planning
- In 2024, RCR published "AI deployment fundamentals for medical imaging"
- Validated AI tools must be integrated so reporting is more efficient and safer
- AI-generated content must be clearly identified within the report
- ALL AI-derived contributions must be reviewed, verified, and approved by a
  named human author who is responsible for the final report
- Employers must provide adequate AI training

Remote Reporting (Including Teleradiology):
- Remote reporting = reporting at distance from where examination was performed
- Arrangements must be developed with wider service planning frameworks
- Remote reporting must not compromise quality compared to in-hospital settings
- Local/regional network reporting must comply with same technical specifications
  and governance as provider-based reporting
- Remote reporters must have access to clinical documentation and imaging records
- Where reporter unfamiliar with local care pathways, mechanisms must support
  contextual understanding (liaison with clinical teams, MDT participation)
- Local operating policies must include business continuity plans
- Teleradiologists working for private providers may be unfamiliar with local
  patient care pathways - organisations must ensure adequate safeguards

SECTION 5: THE REPORT
---------------------
General Considerations:
- Clinical details should form part of the report
- Interpretation may be incorrect if referral details are deficient, absent, or
  misleading
- Inclusion of referrer's text is recommended (may be relevant for future review)
- Where past treatment impacts image interpretation, treatment history should be
  recorded
- Reporters should maintain professional standards when commenting on referrers,
  patients, hospital environment, or equipment
- If scan performance compromised, relevant/factual/non-judgmental statements
  may be included
- Careful proofreading is vital - prevents serious errors such as:
  * Missed negatives
  * Word substitution
  * Wrong side attribution

Scan Protocol and Technical Detail:
- Including scan protocol/technical detail may be appropriate if it aids
  understanding, justifies parameters, adds value, or details deficiencies

Observations and Findings:
- An observations section should state relevant and pertinent findings
- Incidental findings may be mentioned and explained
- Guidelines and experience should be used to avoid irrelevant incidental findings
  or lists of irrelevant negative observations (these detract from report impact
  and generate unnecessary additional tests)
- ALL images acquired (including planning/scout images and non-target areas) must
  be reviewed for patient safety
- Complex studies (cross-sectional imaging) may focus on target areas but must
  have provision for competent and holistic review of all relevant findings
- Responsibility for reviewing non-target areas lies with BOTH reporter and employer
- Where reporting is limited, employers should consider resource implications of
  secondary review
- If prior imaging is beyond reporter's scope, seek additional radiology review
- If prior imaging unavailable and absence may impact interpretation, document this

Standardised and Structured Reporting:
- Reporting style may vary based on clinical context and referrer's needs
- Should prioritise clarity and clinical relevance
- Structured templates are encouraged where suitable (e.g., RECIST criteria,
  National Cancer Registry data, WHO checklist, trauma reporting)
- Structured or AI-generated formats should NOT be used to imply degree of
  scrutiny that has not taken place
- Templates may be adapted to omit irrelevant fields

Report Conclusion (Impressions/Comments/Opinion):
The conclusion is a KEY component and should be SHORT and ACTIONABLE. Short
reports (radiographs, procedures, ultrasound) may not require a conclusion.

The report conclusion should:
- Directly answer the clinical question and address implied questions
- Be tailored to the named referrer as target readership (primary, secondary, or
  tertiary care), explaining technical terms as necessary
- Avoid unnecessary repetition of report body content
- Convey the sensitivity of the test for the clinical question if needed
- Document limitations in likely sensitivity/specificity using clear, factual,
  non-judgmental language
- Explain areas of uncertainty within the radiology diagnosis or provide ranked
  differential diagnosis
- Aim to provide single or differential diagnosis and advise how differentials
  can be refined
- Clarify if the scan did not address something it needed to, recommend further
  tests or actions
- Highlight where non-imaging results will potentially alter interpretation
- Make clear, specific recommendations
- AVOID blanket phrases such as "clinical correlation advised"
- Further imaging recommendations should be used judiciously
- Further tests should add value to overall patient care; referrers should be
  involved in deciding their need
- Follow guidelines for incidental findings; reference or explain when further
  action not needed
- Clinically important incidental findings must be communicated appropriately

Interventional Radiology Reporting and Procedural Description:
- Reports written and communicated promptly following procedure completion
- Subsequent diagnostic conclusions captured separately or as addendum
- Comply with clinical radiology subspecialties and SIG guidance
- If local practice does not obtain written consent, verbal consent and
  complications discussed must form part of the report
- Include instructions on aftercare or need for follow-up procedures/imaging
- Document relevant narrative of intervention, including:
  * Drug administration
  * Efficacy and complications
  * How efficacy should be assessed
- Report layout may vary by procedure type
- Pro formas should be considered for data completeness

Patient Access:
- Improved access to health information enables patient partnership in managing
  their health
- Referrer who requested the test is responsible for reviewing, acting on, and
  communicating the result
- Timelines for patient access should be short, with provision for appropriate
  communication of findings
- When accessed by patient, reports should include standard phrase indicating
  the report has been sent to the referring clinician who referred the patient

SECTION: CONCLUSION
-------------------
The standards provide a baseline that all radiology reports should achieve, so
that quality and consistency of imaging interpretation can be assured. Adherence
ensures image interpretation continues to add value to patient diagnostic and
treatment pathways.

RELEVANCE FOR RADIOLOGICAL MEDILEGAL RESEARCH
This is THE key UK regulatory document that will be cited in court proceedings
involving radiology reporting standards. The 2025 edition specifically addresses:
- Responsibility for reviewing non-target areas (shared between reporter and employer)
- Impact of deficient/absent/misleading referral details on interpretation
- AI-assisted reporting and human responsibility for AI-derived content
- Remote reporting safeguards
- Specific requirements for report conclusions (avoiding "clinical correlation
  advised" as a blanket phrase)
The document establishes the expected standard of care and provides clear
frameworks for liability assignment in reporting-related negligence claims.
