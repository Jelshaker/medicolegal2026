KEY THEMES ACROSS ALL SOURCES
==============================

1. MISSED/DELAYED DIAGNOSIS
   - Consistently the #1 cause of radiology claims
   - Tumours (primary and recurrent) most common missed findings
   - Fractures, cauda equina, perforations, obstructions also common
   - Incidental findings present special challenges

2. REPORTING ERRORS
   - Error rates: 3-5% (standard), 8.7-12.7% (remote)
   - Misdiagnosis (benign/malignant confusion)
   - Missed negatives, word substitution, wrong side
   - Remote reporting increases error risk

3. CONSENT ISSUES
   - Montgomery decision transformed consent law
   - Verbal consent must be documented
   - Material risks must be disclosed (even 1:1,000)
   - Contrast administration and interventional procedures need specific consent

4. REMOTE/TELERADIOLOGY RISKS
   - IT integration challenges between trusts
   - Communication barriers with referring clinicians
   - Lack of access to previous imaging and clinical context
   - Increased error rates with off-hours and remote work
   - Need for robust escalation/referral pathways

5. MEDICAL RECORD REQUIREMENTS
   - Reports must be accurate, complete, contemporaneous
   - Version control for amendments
   - Retention periods (varies by jurisdiction)
   - Central evidence in any litigation

6. INCIDENTAL FINDINGS
   - 37% of claims in Halligan study cited incidental findings
   - RCR Standards acknowledge shared responsibility for non-target review
   - Tension between thoroughness and practicality
   - Screening implications of universal incidentaloma detection

7. FINANCIAL EXPOSURE
   - Claims: £4,000 to £2+ million
   - Legal costs often 5x damages
   - Average settled case: >£50,000
   - One GMC case: >£65,000 in solicitor costs alone

8. PROFESSIONAL RESPONSIBILITIES
   - Follow RCR standards
   - Maintain competence and CPD
   - Ensure proper indemnification
   - Duty of candour when things go wrong
   - Clear, unambiguous reporting

9. REFERRAL QUALITY
   - Poor clinical detail leads to misinterpretation
   - Non-medical referrers increasingly common
   - Vague referrals (e.g., "abdominal pain ? cause") problematic
   - Clinical context inseparable from interpretation

10. LEGAL FRAMEWORKS
    - Bolam/Bolitho test (UK) for standard of care
    - Montgomery test (UK) for consent
    - "Pure diagnosis" trend threatening Bolam protection
    - 3-year limitation period (with "date of knowledge" complications)
    - Human rights considerations (Article 2, 6, 8)
