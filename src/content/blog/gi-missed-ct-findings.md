## The GI blind spots

Gastrointestinal radiology is, counter-intuitively, one of the most fertile
areas for delay-in-diagnosis claims. The reasons are technical: bowel wall
thickening, mesenteric oedema and early ischaemic change are contrast- and
phase-dependent, and a scan performed in the wrong portal venous phase or without
the appropriate reconstruction may be genuinely non-diagnostic for the question
asked.

## Verifiability again

The expert's first task is to establish whether the finding that was missed was
demonstrable on the images as acquired and reviewed on the date in question. This
is a technical question before it is a conduct question.

Where a thickened segment of bowel is demonstrable in the arterial phase that the
department protocol did not routinely acquire, the omission may be a protocol
decision rather than a reporting error — and the analysis must reflect that.

## Request checklist for referrers

When a GI delay claim is contemplated, ask for:

1. the full scan-phase and reconstruction log,
2. the contemporaneous report and the images side by side,
3. a statement of whether a different, standard protocol would have been expected.
