**Shaheen v Daish**

[2025] EWHC 3288 (KB)

## Issue

responsibility for follow-through on imaging requisitions — and whether a patient who never attends contributes negligently to the delay.

## Facts

a GP issued an electronic walk-in chest X-ray referral without clearly communicating its significance; the scan was never performed, delaying a lung cancer diagnosis. The defence alleged contributory negligence for non-attendance.

## Principle

the court rejected contributory negligence — clinicians must clearly inform patients when an unperformed imaging request carries serious diagnostic risks. A walk-in referral issued without explanation does not transfer responsibility to the patient. Consistent with the RCR Standards extract (*ppqi_reporting-standards-guidance.pdf*): providers must maintain procedures for report acknowledgement, communication of unexpected findings and fail-safe notification. See also *Darnley* ([case-law index 13](/radiology-legal-cases#triage-delay)). Practical consequence: record what the patient was told about the reason for the scan, the timescale, and what to do if it is not performed.

## Why it matters — radiology

the unperformed request is a system failure, not a reporting error — and lung cancer is its costliest form. Shaheen moves the duty upstream: the request is not complete when sent, but when the patient understands it and the system confirms completion.

## For consultant radiologists

own the fail-safe end — track unperformed urgent referrals, escalate non-attendance on suspected-cancer pathways, and close the loop back to the referrer rather than assuming attendance.

## For legal professionals

contributory negligence will fail where the significance of attendance was never communicated. Disclose the referral trail, the instructions given, and fail-safe logs — attendance records alone will not decide it.
