**KJY v University College London Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust**

[2023] EWHC 2719 (KB)

## Issue

duty to examine the "whole picture" on a plain radiograph — including incidental pathology outside the referral question.

## Facts

an infant underwent abdominal X-rays for suspected bowel rotation. The gastrointestinal tract was non-pathological, but the films incidentally demonstrated hip malalignment which went unreported. Developmental dysplasia of the hips (DDH) was diagnosed at 14 months. The Trust admitted breach on the failure to report the hips.

## Principle

radiologists have a legal duty to review the entire image and report obvious incidental pathology even where it falls outside the referral question. The RCR Standards (3rd ed, October 2025 — reviewed from the *ppqi_reporting-standards-guidance.pdf* extract) now frame this as shared responsibility: the report should "allow incidental findings to be mentioned" with "provision to ensure competent and holistic review of all relevant findings," responsibility lying with both reporter and employer. *Halligan et al. (Clin Radiol 2026)* — reviewed from the *medicolegalcourtcase.pdf* extract — supplies the empirical context: 41 of 112 abdominal claims (37%) cited incidental pathology, the commonest being missed tumour (23 of 41); only 12 of 41 (29%) were judged substandard. KJY sits on the liability side of that cohort because the hip signs were obvious, not inconspicuous. But where unanticipated pathology is genuinely inconspicuous without a steer from the referrer, *Bolam* / *Hunter v Hanley* still protects a reasonable miss — the nuance the 2025 Standards' wording on deficient or misleading referrals is designed to capture. IR(ME)R duties on referrers to supply adequate clinical information reinforce the point. Applies equally in Scotland.

## Why it matters — radiology

every plain film is a whole-image examination, not just the referred region. KJY converts the RCR's "holistic review" expectation into a liability rule for obvious incidentals — paediatric hips on abdominal views being the paradigm.

## For consultant radiologists

adopt a systematic search pattern that includes included joints and soft tissues, and report obvious incidentals with a clear recommendation and escalation. Where reporting is outsourced or AI-triaged, confirm the service contract covers non-target review — the 2025 Standards place that duty on the employer too.

## For legal professionals

KJY lowers the cost of proving breach where the incidental was obvious, but Halligan bounds it — only 29% of incidental allegations were judged substandard. Test obviousness, not mere presence, and check the referral quality: deficient clinical details are now expressly recognised in the Standards.
