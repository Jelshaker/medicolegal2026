OVERVIEW

This is THE leading UK practical guide to clinical negligence law. Edited by
Andrew Buchan, a barrister at Cloisters Chambers, and contributed to by leading
medical negligence practitioners from Cloisters, 39 Essex Chambers, Doughty Street
Chambers, and Fieldfisher. The book covers the complete legal framework for
clinical negligence claims in England and Wales, with detailed procedural guidance.

CHAPTER 5: THE DUTY OF CARE
----------------------------
Covers:
- Establishment of duty of care in clinical contexts
- Relationship between clinician and patient
- Scope of duty for healthcare professionals
- Non-delegable duty of care
- Hospitals' and healthcare providers' duties
- Clinical commissioning groups' responsibilities
- Duty of care in diagnostic imaging contexts

KEY POINTS:
- Duty of care is the foundational element of clinical negligence
- All healthcare professionals owe a duty of care to their patients
- The duty extends to diagnostic activities including radiology
- Employers have non-delegable duties for patient safety

CHAPTER 6: BREACH OF DUTY AND STRICT LIABILITY
-----------------------------------------------
Covers:
- The standard of care expected
- Bolam test and its application
- Bolitho addition to Bolam (logical basis requirement)
- Standard of care for specialists (including radiologists)
- Strict liability in certain circumstances
- Res ipsa loquitur in clinical contexts
- Clinical guidelines and their evidential value
- NICE guidelines and their role

KEY POINTS:
- Radiologists are held to the standard of a reasonably competent consultant radiologist
- Clinical guidelines (including RCR Standards) are strong evidence of standard of care
- Bolam test remains central but Bolitho requires logical basis
- Following guidelines does not automatically preclude breach

CHAPTER 7: CAUSED AND DAMAGE
-----------------------------
Covers:
- Causation test: "but for" test
- Material contribution to damage
- "Taken into account" test for diagnostic delay
- Loss of chance doctrine
- Apportionment of damages
- Multiple defendants
- Contribution between defendants
- Head of damages: special and general damages

KEY POINTS:
- Causation is often THE critical issue in radiology claims
- "But for" test: would the outcome have been different had the abnormality been correctly diagnosed?
- Loss of chance may apply when earlier diagnosis would have improved outcomes
- The "material contribution" test may apply where multiple causes

CHAPTER 9: THE PROOF OF NEGLIGENCE
------------------------------------
Covers:
- Burden of proof (on claimant)
- Standard of proof (balance of probabilities)
- Evidence required to prove negligence
- Expert evidence requirements
- How courts assess expert evidence
- The "battle of the experts"
- Single joint expert reports
- Duty of experts to the court

KEY POINTS:
- Claimant bears burden of proving all elements of negligence
- Evidence must show breach AND causation
- Expert evidence is central to clinical negligence cases
- Experts must give independent, impartial evidence to the court
- Courts are wary of partisan expert evidence

CHAPTER 10: CONSENT
-------------------
Covers:
- Legal basis for consent
- Bolam test for disclosure of risks (pre-Montgomery)
- Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board (2015) - GAME CHANGER
- Montgomery test: material risks a reasonable person in the patient's position
  would attach significance to, OR risks the particular patient would attach significance to
- Doctor's own views about risks
- Therapeutic exception
- Consent for diagnostic procedures (including contrast administration)
- Consent for interventional radiology
- Documentation of consent
- Minors and consent
- Adults lacking capacity

KEY MONTCGOMERY PRINCIPLES:
- Doctor must inform patient of material risks
- "Material risk" = risk that a reasonable person in patient's position would
  consider significant, OR that THIS patient would consider significant
- Doctor must discuss with patient any special concerns they express
- Documented consent is essential
- Montgomery fundamentally changed the law on consent disclosure

KEY MEDILEGAL PRINCIPLES FOR RADIOLOGY PRACTICE
------------------------------------------------

1. DUTY OF CARE
   - Radiologists owe duty to patients they image
   - This includes proper interpretation and reporting
   - Duty extends to advising on further investigations

2. BREACH OF DUTY
   - Assessed by Bolam/Bolitho test
   - Standard of competent consultant radiologist
   - RCR Standards are evidence of expected standard
   - Following guidelines does not guarantee no breach found

3. CAUSATION
   - Most contested element in radiology claims
   - "But for" test: would outcome have been different?
   - Loss of chance doctrine relevant in cancer screening/delay cases
   - Material contribution test may apply

4. CONSENT (Montgomery)
   - Must disclose material risks
   - Risks material to THIS patient must be discussed
   - Documentation essential
   - Even small risks (1:1,000) must be addressed if material
   - Applies to contrast administration and interventional procedures

5. MEDICAL RECORDS
   - Radiology reports are critical evidence
   - Must be contemporaneous, accurate, complete
   - Amendments must be properly documented
   - Version control for electronic reports

6. EXPERT EVIDENCE
   - Central to both sides of claim
   - Must be independent and impartial
   - Reports must follow prescribed format
   - Radiology experts must be at consultant level

7. HUMAN RIGHTS
   - Article 2 may be engaged in death cases
   - Article 8 relevant to patient confidentiality
   - Article 6 for procedural fairness

8. LIMITATION
   - 3 years from act/omission or date of knowledge
   - Date of knowledge often disputed
   - Section 33 discretion may extend period
