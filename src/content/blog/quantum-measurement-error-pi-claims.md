## Introduction

A significant minority of orthopaedic personal-injury and clinical-negligence
claims turn not on whether an injury exists, but on its *quantification* — the
degree of malalignment, the volume of residual fragment, the thickness of a
cartilage defect. These are imaging measurements, and they are only as good as
their reproducibility.

## Sources of measurement error

Three errors recur in my quantum work:

1. **Static calibre vs. calibrated scale.** A measurement performed without a
   calibration object validated against the PACS geometry can drift by several
   percent between workstations.
2. **Window/level dependence.** Soft-tissue and bone measurements are
   sensitive to windowing; reporting this makes re-derivation possible.
3. **Plane obliquity.** A knee radiograph taken in slight rotation will shorten
   the apparent joint-line measurement, and that shortening can be outsized
   relative to the real deformity.

## What the indemnified expert should record

My standard for measurement-based opinions is that every figure is accompanied
by:

- the modality, plane and slice/anatomical landmark used,
- the measurement tool and calibration applied,
- a statement of intra-observer variability quoted from the literature,
- the assumption the figure holds for the clinical estimation of loss.

Where I disagree with the opposing expert's figure, the disagreement should be
localised to the geometry, not to a competing "estimate". That discipline keeps
quantum disputes within the imaging and out of the advocacy.
