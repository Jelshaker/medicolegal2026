**Brady v Southend University Hospital NHS Foundation Trust**

[2020] EWHC 289 (QB)

## Issue

whether an incorrect diagnostic interpretation on imaging automatically constitutes negligence.

## Facts

radiologists reporting CT scans identified an abdominal mass as omental infarction rather than severe infection, delaying treatment.

## Principle

an incorrect opinion is not automatically negligent where the reasoning was supported by a responsible body of medical opinion (Bolam/Bolitho) and expressed with appropriate caution. Claim dismissed.

## Why it matters — radiology

abdominal CT forces genuine differential diagnoses — infarct versus infection, ischaemia versus inflammation — where reasonable radiologists differ. Brady is the counterweight to Muller and Penney: it protects honest, reasoned interpretation from hindsight.

## For consultant radiologists

the protection depends on the report showing its working — differential stated, uncertainty expressed, and follow-up or escalation advised. A bare, confident wrong answer earns no Bolam shelter; a reasoned, cautious one may.

## For legal professionals

test Brady claims on the reasoning, not the outcome. If the report documents a logical differential supported by contemporaneous findings, the claimant must go further and show the reasoning itself was illogical (Bolitho) — outcome error alone is insufficient.
