## **Wright v. MetroSouth Medical Center (Over the pond)**

**Cook County Circuit Court, Illinois, United States**  
**Jury verdict: $8.125 million**

### Facts and imaging

In August 2013, 62-year-old Althea Wright attended the emergency department at MetroSouth Medical Center (Blue Island, Illinois) with fatigue and shortness of breath. An emergency physician requested a chest radiograph.

- **Initial review:** The attending radiologist reported the radiograph as normal. Evidence at trial established that the radiologist spent **approximately 80 seconds** reviewing the study and later cited fatigue as the most likely explanation for missing the abnormality.
- **Delay and progression:** No follow-up imaging was arranged. Over a year later (October 2014), repeat chest radiography and CT demonstrated a mass in the same location. What had been a potentially curable Stage I squamous cell carcinoma had progressed to an inoperable Stage III tumour with nodal spread. Ms Wright died in March 2015 aged 64.

### Outcome and significance

A Cook County jury awarded **$8.125 million** to Ms Wright's estate and family. The case is a stark reminder that PACS audit logs and viewing durations can become central exhibits in litigation, and that workload or fatigue will not excuse a failure to meet the standard of reasonable care.
