OVERVIEW
--------
This document presents an analysis by Medical Protection (MPS), a UK medical
defence organisation with over 130 years of experience defending doctors and
healthcare professionals. The analysis examined nearly 800 cases involving
UK-based radiologists, covering clinical negligence claims, complaints, GMC
investigations, inquests, and medicolegal advice requests. The majority of
clinical negligence claims arise from private practice, while other case types
typically arise from both NHS and private settings.

KEY STATISTICS
--------------
- Nearly 800 total cases analysed (2016-mid 2023)
- ~200 clinical negligence claims from private radiology practice
- ~600+ other cases (complaints, GMC investigations, inquests, disciplinary)
- ~200 report-writing cases (Trust SI investigations, statements for claims)
- Highest radiology negligence claim (reporting error): >£400,000 total
- One GMC/disciplinary case solicitor cost: >£65,000

COMMON CLAIMS THEMES (Private Practice - ~200 cases)
-----------------------------------------------------
1. Complications arising from interventional radiology procedures
2. Failure to obtain informed consent
3. Failure to request additional views (e.g., slipped upper femoral epiphysis)
4. Errors or discrepancies in reporting:
   a. Misdiagnosis of an abnormality (benign lesion reported as malignant, or vice versa)
   b. Missed abnormalities including:
      - Primary tumours
      - Metastases
      - Fractures
      - Anastomotic leak
      - Stroke
      - Subarachnoid haemorrhage
      - Subdural haematoma
      - Cauda equina syndrome
      - Multiple myeloma
      - Epidural abscess
      - Bowel perforation

COMMON COMPLAINTS THEMES (~100 complaints analysed)
---------------------------------------------------
1. Missed or delayed diagnosis from interpretation errors
2. Inappropriate physical contact or examination
3. Poor manner and attitude, including lack of empathy
4. Inadequate consent for procedures
5. Complications post-biopsy (e.g., bleeding)
6. Incorrect biopsy sample obtained (e.g., kidney rather than liver)
7. Nerve damage following insertion of lines

INQUEST ISSUES (~90 inquests)
-----------------------------
1. Missed or delayed diagnosis from reporting errors:
   - Missed subarachnoid and subdural haemorrhage
   - Cases where anticoagulation was given after missed haemorrhage, causing further bleeding
2. Death during or following interventional radiology procedures (biopsies):
   - Haemorrhage
   - Air embolus
3. Missed leaking abdominal aortic aneurysm
4. Missed pulmonary embolus

CRIMINAL CASES
--------------
A small number of criminal cases, primarily relating to allegations of
inappropriate examination of patients.

REPORT WRITING CASES (~200 cases)
---------------------------------
1. Errors in reporting, including missed diagnosis (tumours, subarachnoid
   haemorrhage, fractures)
2. Performing a biopsy on the incorrect side
3. Complications of intervention procedures (biopsy injury to other structures)
4. Declining to perform imaging
5. Inappropriate examination and alleged sexual assault of patients
6. Delays in diagnosis (e.g., cauda equina)
7. Requests for court reports on suspected non-accidental injury

REGULATORY (GMC) AND DISCIPLINARY CASES
---------------------------------------
Clinical competence concerns:
- Reporting of images and interventional procedures
- Inadequate supervision of trainees
- Competence to perform interventional radiology procedures

Non-clinical concerns:
- Inappropriate examination of patients
- Allegations of domestic violence
- Failure to obtain car tax/insurance
- Writing prescriptions for self or family
- Inappropriate touching or sexual assault allegations
- Drink driving
- Conducting private work while off sick from NHS
- Claiming waiting list initiative payments when not entitled
- Accessing medical records inappropriately
- Failing to declare previous investigations/dismissal on applications

REMOTE RADIOLOGY REPORTING - KEY FINDINGS
-----------------------------------------
An increasing number of claims and cases associated with outsourced remote
radiology reporting. Key problems identified:

1. IT Systems:
   - Many systems don't allow image sharing between hospital trusts
   - Previous imaging unavailable to reporting radiologist
   - Exposes reporting radiologist to risk
   - May result in unnecessary imaging

2. Communication:
   - Radiology request forms may lack important clinical detail
   - Face-to-face communication unavailable for remote reporters
   - Loss of communication with other healthcare professionals
   - No traditional MDT for remote reporters
   - Difficulty contacting referring clinician
   - Reports may be interpreted incorrectly

ERROR RATES IN REMOTE REPORTING:
- Standard error rate: 3-5% for plain film radiographs
- Higher for cross-sectional imaging
- Offsite reporting error rate: 8.7% to 12.7% (2017 study)
- Reporting during nighttime/above contracted hours increases error risk

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR REMOTE RADIOLOGISTS
---------------------------------------
1. Ensure report wording is unambiguous
2. Use plain English, avoid acronyms/abbreviations/colloquialisms
3. Explain unusual medical terminology
4. Review relevant previous radiology when available
5. State when previous imaging is unavailable
6. Consider whether knowledge of results would change the report
7. Comply with local escalation/referral mechanisms for significant abnormalities
8. Ensure robust reporting pathways exist before accepting remote work
9. Consider increased risk when working above contracted hours or at night
10. Ensure contracted remote reporting companies provide quality assurance
11. Participate in radiology events and learning meetings (REALMs)
12. Ensure CPD and discrepancy reporting per RCR standards

TOP TIPS TO MINIMISE RISK (General)
-----------------------------------
1. Keep clinical management updated and aligned with current best practice
2. Ensure accurate, clear, unambiguous documentation
3. Include recommendations for referring clinicians
4. Set out areas of uncertainty in interpretation
5. Review and consider relevant previous radiology
6. Comply with local escalation/referral policies for significant abnormalities
7. Follow RCR standards and guidance
8. Discuss benefits, risks, and no-treatment options with patients
9. Listen to patients and explore their concerns
10. Explain frequent and serious complications (even if rare)
11. Treat colleagues with respect, maintain good communication
12. Have all necessary information for remote consultations
13. Give clear cost information for private patients
14. Be open and honest when things go wrong
15. Apologise and explain when errors occur
16. Ensure full indemnification for all work conducted in the UK
17. Contact MPS about reporting patients based in other countries

RELEVANCE FOR RADIOLOGICAL MEDILEGAL RESEARCH
This source provides extensive UK-specific data on radiology claims, complaints,
and regulatory issues. It is particularly valuable for understanding the common
types of errors that lead to litigation, the financial costs involved, and the
specific risks associated with remote/teleradiology reporting. The data on missed
diagnoses, reporting errors, and informed consent issues provides a strong
empirical foundation for a medicolegal paper on radiology.
