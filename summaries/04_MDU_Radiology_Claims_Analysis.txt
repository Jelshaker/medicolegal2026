OVERVIEW
--------
This article by the Medical Defence Union (MDU) analyses clinical negligence
claims notified by radiology members working in the independent/private sector.
It provides insights into claim outcomes, common reasons for claims, and risk
management advice for radiologists.

KEY STATISTICS
--------------
- Analysis based on 200 recent claims against consultant radiologists in the
  independent sector who are MDU members
- Nearly 3/4 of claims successfully defended (compensation paid in ~1/4 of cases)
- Settlement costs varied from £4,000 to almost £2 million
- Majority of settled cases cost well in excess of £50,000
- Legal costs can exceed the amount of damages paid (notably: one case with
  damages >£100,000 but legal costs significantly exceeding this)

COMPENSATION PRINCIPLES
-----------------------
- Compensation levels bear little/no relation to seriousness of clinical allegations
- Instead, compensation reflects the cost of restoring the claimant to the position
  they would have been in if the negligence had not occurred
- Large payments for patients fund future care and compensate for loss of earnings
- Even lower value claims can settle for damages and costs beyond most members'
  means to pay

OUTCOMES OF CASES (Non-settled claims)
---------------------------------------
Cases not settled were either:
1. Discontinued by claimant - MDU successfully argued:
   - Liability rested with treating clinicians rather than radiology member
   - Radiologist's interpretation was reasonable given clinical information
   - Interpretation would not have altered patient outcome
2. Statute barred (out of time):
   - Claimant must bring claim within 3 years from date of incident or date of
     knowledge of alleged harm
   - For children with capacity: limitation period begins at age 18 (16 in Scotland)
   - No time limit for patients who lack capacity
3. Some closed after MDU successfully argued member not sufficiently involved
   in clinical sequence of events

COMMON REASONS FOR CLAIMS
--------------------------
1. DELAYED DIAGNOSIS (most common)
   - Cancer accounted for ~25% of delayed diagnosis cases
   - Fractures commonly resulting in claims
   - Cauda equina syndrome commonly resulting in claims
   - Argument: if abnormality had been recognised/reported correctly, patient
     would have received sooner treatment, reducing disability
   - Allegations centre on:
     * Abnormality NOT detected
     * Abnormality picked up but MISINTERPRETED, leading to unnecessary/incorrect treatment

2. MEDICATION ISSUES (small number but high cost)
   - Allergic reactions
   - Wrong medication provided
   - Contrast-related claim in child settled for >£100,000 with substantial costs

3. COMMUNICATION SKILLS
   - Cases focused on doctor's communication skills
   - Often related to obtaining appropriate consent for radiological procedures

4. INTERVENTIONAL RADIOLOGY
   - Substantial interventional element in some claims
   - Some expensive to settle
   - Carries more perceived risks
   - Less commonly carried out than general radiology
   - Members may be more aware of risks and justifiably cautious

5. MODALITY BREAKDOWN
   - ~25% of claims involved MRI scan allegations
   - Similar proportion involved plain X-ray allegations
   - ~10% of cases followed patient death

RISK MANAGEMENT ADVICE
----------------------
1. Communication and Results:
   - Ensure robust procedures for communicating results to relevant parties
   - Ensure appropriate and prompt follow-up and treatment

2. Consent:
   - Be aware of GMC guidance on consent and other relevant guidelines (e.g., NICE)
   - Consent should be obtained by appropriate team member, ideally by the
     radiologist undertaking the procedure
   - Patient must be aware of risks, benefits, complications, and therapeutic options
   - Document these discussions carefully
   - Patient leaflets and information sheets should be used and documented

3. Medication and Contrast:
   - Consider patient's past medical history, medication history, and allergies
     before administering pre-procedural medication and contrast media

4. Interventional Procedures:
   - Ensure appropriate handover to recovery staff for interventional procedures
     or those under sedation
   - Have system to verify right site and patient; cross-check against referral

5. Reporting Environment:
   - Keep interruptions in reporting room to minimum

6. Unexpected Findings:
   - Consider contacting referring doctor if unexpected finding requiring urgent
     investigation is found

7. Examinations:
   - Offer chaperone for intimate examinations (e.g., pelvic ultrasound)

8. When Things Go Wrong:
   - Be open and honest with patient
   - Provide explanation of what happened and likely short/long term effects
   - Say sorry
   - Get advice from MDU regarding duty of candour requirements

CASE STUDY: Ruptured Achilles Tendon
------------------------------------
Facts:
- Patient developed pre-Achilles tendon bursitis, referred for steroid injection
- Radiologist performed successful ultrasound-guided injection
- Three months later, symptoms recurred; patient seen by rheumatologist
- Patient returned to radiologist who found persistent left pre-Achilles bursitis
- Second injection performed (deep to paratenon of Achilles tendon)
- Patient suffered full thickness Achilles rupture shortly after procedure

Allegations:
- Rupture resulted from injecting local anaesthetic and steroid around tendon
  on two occasions
- Patient not warned about / not aware of risk of tendon rupture before either
  procedure

Expert Opinion (MDU-appointed):
- Procedure was relatively safe
- Member's treatment was reasonable and logical
- Published evidence shows risk of rupture ~1:1,000
- Patient referred by senior consultant rheumatologist who also advised this was
  appropriate course
- NO evidence in ultrasound reports that patient was consented for rupture risk
- Radiologist claimed verbal warning given but NOT documented

Outcome:
- MDU denied liability on member's behalf
- Patient's solicitors issued proceedings alleging no consent obtained
- Radiologist agreed verbal warnings were given but not documented
- Claim settled WITHOUT admission of liability
- Damages: £12,500
- Legal costs: almost 5x damages amount (~£62,500)

KEY LESSONS FROM CASE STUDY
-----------------------------
1. Verbal consent discussions must be DOCUMENTED
2. Even small risks (1:1,000) must be specifically addressed in consent
3. Lack of documentation can lead to settlement even when liability is denied
4. Legal costs can dramatically exceed damages (5x in this case)
5. Even successfully defended claims can result in significant financial exposure

RELEVANCE FOR RADIOLOGICAL MEDILEGAL RESEARCH
This MDU article provides valuable data on:
1. Claim outcomes in the independent sector (75% successfully defended)
2. The financial exposure radiologists face (even defended claims have high costs)
3. The statute of limitations framework for medical negligence claims
4. The specific conditions most commonly associated with radiology claims
5. The critical importance of documented consent
6. The disproportionate cost of legal proceedings relative to damages
7. The case study illustrates the documentation gap that can undermine an
   otherwise defensible clinical position
The article complements the MPS data by providing an independent sector perspective
and specific practical risk management advice.
