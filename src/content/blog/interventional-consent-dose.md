## Why interventional claims differ

Interventional radiology sits at the junction of a diagnostic specialty and a
procedure-based one. The medicolegal questions therefore span both: did the
imaging justify the intervention, and did the intervention itself meet the
standard of care?

## The consent conversation

Informed consent in interventional practice should cover the indication, the
alternatives (including conservative management and surgery), and the material
risks — including radiation dose, sedation and the specific procedural risks.
Under the "reasonable patient" standard the conversation should be documented,
not merely the consent form signed.

## Dose and disclosure

A documented dose report, imaging time and fluoroscopy minutes are
contemporaneous evidence of what was done. When a complication does occur, the
duty of candour applies: the patient should be told promptly and honestly, and
the case should be reviewed.

For the instructing solicitor, the practical request list is short:

1. the full dose and procedure log,
2. the contemporaneous consent record,
3. contemporaneous post-procedure review notes.
