**Brady v Southend University Hospital NHS Foundation Trust**  
**[2020] EWHC 158 (QB)**

Following a 2013 appendicectomy, the claimant developed persistent abdominal pain. Initial CT imaging revealed a right upper-quadrant mass interpreted as likely omental infarction. Despite continuing symptoms and a subsequent enlarging mass, biopsy was deferred in favour of conservative management. The claimant later developed an _Actinomyces_ psoas abscess, arguing that an earlier biopsy would have enabled prompt diagnosis and treatment.

The court dismissed the claim, ruling the original CT interpretation was clinically reasonable and deferring biopsy fell within responsible practice. The judgment confirms that _Bolam_, subject to _Bolitho_’s logical scrutiny, applies equally to diagnostic interpretation. A subsequent diagnosis does not retrospectively establish negligence; breach requires proof that the original interpretation or management was not reasonably open to competent practitioners given the contemporaneous clinical and imaging context. Cautious wording in reports does not shield fundamentally indefensible interpretations, though it does not create negligence where differential diagnosis is genuinely ambiguous.

Radiological reports must clearly communicate findings, diagnostic confidence, and appropriately indicated follow-up. When assessing alleged diagnostic negligence, expert evidence should systematically address what the original imaging demonstrated, whether the interpretation was defensible, whether further investigation was then warranted, and whether any breach likely altered the outcome. Crucially, the existence of a hindsight-revealing test does not establish that competent clinicians were required to order it at the time.
